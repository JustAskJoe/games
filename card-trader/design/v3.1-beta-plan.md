## The short version
1. **You're an expert, not a tourist.** Sets and rarities are always known: drop unreadable cards, translation and fluency. The real uncertainty moves to **condition**.
2. **Condition is a range, grading is the gamble.** Your loupe gives a range ("8–9"), not an exact grade. Tools and skill narrow it, never to certain. The slab is the truth. The Black Label 10 is the rare perfect card.
3. **Getting slabs back is an event.** A grader shipment box you open, slabs face down, flip to reveal, checked against your guess.
4. **The inventory explains itself.** A guided day 1, fewer places to put things, and defaults that do the boring part.
5. **A QoL sweep:** auto-sleeve, auto-file, "Stock the floor", sort and filter, warnings that tell you what to do.
6. **Fix what lies or dominates:** reviews that make things up, a rating nobody can read, the scalper as the best strategy, tells as noise.
7. **Give days 4–7 a reason to exist,** and make vintage reachable.

## 1. Knowledge: you are an informed card owner
**Now:** cards from games you didn't grow up on show as "(unreadable Starweave card)" with no rarity. You read three of a game, or pay the 5₵ translator, to become fluent. Both Claude runs and Hunter flagged this as wrong for a shop owner.

**Change:**
- **Always known:** the card's name, set, era, rarity, printing (first edition, reverse, alt art, and so on) and the market price for each grade. That's a price guide plus experience.
- **Still unknown until you look:** condition (section 2), fakes and trims (UV, Spectrometer, the graders), and misprints you can only see with the Print Lens.
- **Remove:** `readable()`, `FLUENT_AT`, `G.fluency`, the translator fee, the `read` loupe tool, and the "home game" reading perk.
  - **Q1:** keep "the game you grew up on" as an opening answer, but give it a different perk (+1 condition step on that game's cards, or a small discount from its distributor)?
- **Sellers keep their blind spots.** A Puffi kid still under-prices a card from another species' game. The edge stays; it moves from "I can read it" to "I know what it's worth and they don't".

**Engine touch points:** `readable`, `estimate()` (`rar` is always known), `sellerBelief` (unchanged), `inspect` (`read` goes), UI `estText`. The sim bots already use `estimate()`, so they follow automatically; rerun the Balance band, because knowing every rarity makes buying easier.

## 2. Condition is a range; grading is the gamble
**Now:**
- One loupe look reveals the exact centering, edges and surface, so `trueGrade()` is exact.
- Halo grades exactly; QuikSlab is ±1.
- Nothing is uncertain once you've looked, so sending to the graders is just paying a fee for a known multiplier.
- The first estimate assumes grade 7, which is how a "498₵" card became 280₵ after the loupe.

**Change: your eye gives ranges.**
- Each subgrade (centering, edges, surface) shows as a **range** after a loupe look: for example, edges 7–9.
- The card's overall grade shows as a range ("grade 8–9"), with value as a range ("140–220₵"). The headline number is the low end, so nobody gets overexcited.
- **Before any look**, the card shows the range for its source: pack-fresh 7–10, binder 5–9, shoebox 2–7, and so on. That's honest, and it replaces today's perfect-card number.
- **Personal grading skill:** a track you upgrade at close, replacing or folding in today's Lens and Hands.

  | Level | Cost | Range width per subgrade | Note |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | Eye 0 (start) | – | ±2 | |
  | Eye 1: Centering card | ~40₵ | centering ±1 | |
  | Eye 2: Edge light | ~60₵ | edges ±1 | |
  | Eye 3: Surface lamp | ~80₵ | surface ±1 | |
  | Eye 4: "Trained eye" | ~120₵, from day 5 | every subgrade ±1, with the true value more likely in the middle | |

  - **Never certain:** even at Eye 4, the range is at least two grades wide on about one card in three, and the true grade sits at the edge of your range about 20% of the time.
  - **Q2:** these widths and prices are first guesses for the Balance agent to tune. OK to replace the Lens and Hands upgrades with this track (keeping the Spectrometer for fakes)?
- **The slab is the truth.** A grader returns the card's true grade. *My reading of your note: "it comes back what it says" means the slab's number is real and final, so the gamble is your read, not the grader.*
  - Halo is exact.
  - QuikSlab is cheaper and faster but its number can be one off. Either keep that as a second gamble, or make it exact but cap it at 9.
  - **Q3:** which QuikSlab rule?
- **Black Label 10.** Only when all three subgrades are a true 10 (perfect), graded by Halo.
  - About 1 in 150 pack-fresh holo-or-better pulls; tune this.
  - Value is about 2.5× a normal 10 (so about 8× raw); tune this.
  - Your eye can never promise it. At best, your range says "9–10 on everything".
  - **Q4:** Halo only, or a third grader (a premium "Obsidian" service: pricey, slow, the only one that issues Black Label)?
- **The decision this creates:** every close, you choose which cards are worth a fee and which to sell raw, from ranges. That's the gamble Hunter wants. Better eye = better bets, never a sure thing.

**Engine touch points:**
- Store a seeded `c.eye` (the persona's read) on each card when it's looked at, so the range doesn't reroll each time.
- `estimate()` returns `{ lo, hi, value: lo }`.
- `resolveGrades()` uses the true grade, and adds `blackLabel` when all subgrades are 10 and the company is Halo (or Obsidian).
- `GRADERS[co].mult` gains a Black Label case.
- Sim: bots use `lo`. Grading choices become real decisions, so the careful bot needs a rule (for example "send if hi ≥ 9 and the fee is under 10% of the hi value").

## 3. Getting slabs back feels great
**Now:** returns show as stamps in the morning report.

**Change: the grader shipment.**
- **Send:** a submission form at close. Drag cards in. Each shows your range and what it's worth at each possible grade (raw / 8 / 9 / 10 / Black Label). Pick a service and see the fee and return day. Submitted cards show in a "📦 At Halo, back day 6" tray.
- **Return:** a taped grader box sits on the back counter that morning, apart from the supply crate.
  1. Tap to slit the tape.
  2. Slabs lie face down in foam.
  3. Flip each one: the card turns over, the label slides in, and the grade stamps down with a sound.
  4. Your guess shows beside it ("You said 8–9 → **9**!" in green, or "→ 7" in red with a sad trombone). The new value ticks up from your low estimate.
  5. **Black Label:** the lights dim, the label is black with gold foil, DJ Vee shouts it, and it goes in the morning report and the run summary.
  6. Fakes and trims flip to a red **FAKE** or **ALTERED** stamp.
- **After:** "Put on show" sends the best slabs straight to the case (they need no sleeve). The rest go to the safe.
- **Day 10 (Grade Day):** the finale opens every remaining box in one sequence before the final score, as the last big beat of the run.

**Build:** UI only, plus a `gradeBox` item in the morning crate. The engine already emits `grade` events with the company and grade.

## 4. The inventory makes sense: guided day 1 and fewer places
**Now (what testers hit):**
- Eight places stock can be: case, counter binder, top-loader binder, rack, back binder, inbox, safe, shelves.
- Two rooms, sleeve rules per fixture, and a shape grid.
- Hunter: "the inventory part is confusing."
- The kid and goblin personas hit 12–13 sleeve refusals a run.
- The min-maxer hit "no room" 8 times without being told the rack holds 16 packs.

**Change, simplify first:**
- **Merge the inbox into the back binder.** New cards land in the back binder, auto-filed and sleeved by your policy. The inbox only exists while unpacking, as an "unsorted" tray that empties itself.
- **The safe fills itself:** slabs and anything over the safe threshold go there automatically. You can still move them.
- **Three things to think about on the floor:** the case (slabs and hard-sleeved cards, lockable), the counter binder (mid cards) and the rack (packs and sealed). The top-loader binder becomes a case upgrade ("a top-loader row in your case"), not a fourth fixture.
- **Every refusal says how to fix it,** with a one-tap fix: "Needs a hard sleeve. [Sleeve it (1 of 7 left)]". "Rack full (16/16). [Put the other 6 on the shelf]".

**Change, a guided day 1 (skippable, and replayable from the menu):** DJ Vee walks one action at a time, highlighting the spot and waiting for you to do it.
1. Open the crate (tap).
2. "This is your back binder. Everything you own lives here until you put it out."
3. Pick your best card, then **Sleeve** (hard), then put it in the case. "The case is for your good stuff. It needs a hard sleeve."
4. Fill two more case slots with the auto button ("Stock the floor", section 5), so they learn the shortcut on day 1.
5. The counter binder: "Mid cards here, penny sleeves are fine."
6. The rack: "Packs and sealed hang here."
7. Tags: "Everything gets a price tag at market +15%. Change it if you like."
8. **OPEN.** The first customer is scripted and easy: a buyer who wants something you just put out, and the "show" button glows.

Later firsts get a single DJ line and a highlight the first time each happens, with no wall of text: first thief, first fake, first sealed collector, first grader return, first estate lot.

## 5. QoL sweep
Defaults that do the boring part. Every automatic thing can be turned off in a small Shop Settings panel.

| What | How it works | Default |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Auto-sleeve** | Placing a card that needs a sleeve uses the cheapest sleeve that fixture accepts. If the drawer's out, it says so with a "Reorder" button. | On |
| **Auto-file** | New cards are filed and sleeved by policy (today's `autoStore` + `applyPolicy`, made automatic). | On |
| **Stock the floor** | One button. Fills empty case slots with your best sleevable cards, the counter binder with mid cards, and the rack from the shelves. Shows a preview: "Will move 5 cards, use 3 hard sleeves." | Button |
| **Auto-rack** | Between customers, refills the rack from shelf packs of the same game. | On |
| **Auto-tag** | Every floor item gets market × your markup (one slider, default +15%). Hand-set tags stick. | On |
| **Sort and filter** | The stock list sorts by value, grade range, game, set or location. Filters: "not on the floor", "worth grading (range hits 9+)", "unsleeved". | – |
| **Find** | Type a card name; it pulses where the card is. | – |
| **Bulk actions** | Select many, then toss to bulk, sleeve, move or send to graders. | – |
| **Warnings bar** | Badges with a fix button: crate unopened, case empty or sparse, rack full or empty, sleeves low, slabs back, a fake in stock found by a later scan. | On |
| **Undo** | The last move in the back room. | – |
| **List view** | A plain sortable list instead of the shape grids, for players who don't want Tetris. The grids stay for the case and the shelves. | Grids |
| **Keyboard** | F floor, B back, S stock the floor, G grading, number keys for counter actions. | – |
| **Counter shortcuts** | "Show best fit" and "Inspect all" buttons; "Inspect all" respects patience. | – |
| **End-of-day sheet** | In and out, best sale, what was stolen, why the rating moved, what's arriving tomorrow. | On |

**Q6:** anything here you don't want automatic? The design question: does auto-everything remove too much of the shop fantasy? My pick: automate filing, sleeving and racking (chores); keep choosing what goes in the case, pricing and grading as the player's job.

## 6. Systems: keep, change, remove, add
From the playtests' verdict lists (`playtests/agents-2026-11-01.md`, "Verdict across all runs"; `playtests/claude-2026-11-01.json`, `verdict`) and the findings page.

### Keep
- **Browsers buying off the floor** (24/24 runs keep).
- **Sealed ordering and the rack** (24/24).
- **The loupe** (21/24), reworked per section 2.
- **Upgrades** (17/24).
- **After Hours nights** (15/24).
- **Thieves with the kid and drifter choices.**
- **DJ Vee warnings that teach a counter:** the Scale Gang led to selling behind the counter, and perfect promos led to the Spectrometer.
- **Gems in cheap lots:** keep them, but smaller and rarer. See the Balance watch-outs: 857₵ for a 4₵ ask, and ~498₵ for 14₵.

### Change
- **The star rating** (clear 1/5 in both Claude runs; change for 7/24).
  - Each morning shows "Rating 4.12 → 4.05" with the top three reasons, with numbers.
  - Reviews may only mention true things (the quick-fix PR handles the lying lines).
  - **Q7:** what does rep do that stars don't? My suggestion: rep is how people treat you (sellers' asks, how often good sellers come), stars are how many walk-ins come and what they'll pay. Rep 43 should feel different from rep 100.
- **The scalper is a dominant strategy** (the min-maxer earned ~1,300₵ from three sales with no felt cost). Options:
  - (a) he takes at most 6 packs;
  - (b) tomorrow's pack buyers drop by what he took (already partly true) and stars dip;
  - (c) "Limit 2" is the default policy, so the scalper gets 2.
  - My pick: (a) + (c), plus a visible "tomorrow's buyers: fewer" in the end-of-day sheet. The Balance agent adds an "always sell to the scalper" bot to prove it.
- **Tells are noise** (most honest customers show one). Cut honest tells from ~12% to ~3% of customers, and make the signature tell of a returning crook stand out (a coloured highlight once they're on the board).
- **Sleeves** (change for 18/24): auto-sleeve (section 5) and a visible payoff. When wear is avoided, say so in the end-of-day sheet ("Hard sleeves saved 3 cards from wear, about 24₵"). Foiled switches already show.
- **Fakes:** keep the rescan loop, but warn when something in stock hasn't been scanned since you bought the Spectrometer ("2 cards never checked with the Spectrometer").
- **Sealed value you can't reach:** let a booster box sit on the rack (as a 2×2 item), or show "walk-in value" next to market value.
- **Allocation and reprints:** show the risk before you order. The catalog marks "allocation tight" and "reprint rumored" per set.
- **Swap meets:** traders sometimes want your mid cards, not always your best.

### Remove
- **Unreadable cards, translation and fluency** (section 1).
- **Repacks, unless they're rebuilt** (never touched by 22/24 runs; the Balance agent found 0 of 48 repacks sold).
  - **Q8:** cut repacks from v3.1 (hide the sorter), or fix the economics first?
- **The board as a separate decision.** Fold it into watching: a returning crook is highlighted automatically, and "refuse" gives a small bonus over watching (no theft risk, and a little rep with honest customers who saw it). Otherwise there's nothing to choose (never touched by 12/24).

### Add
- **A mid-run spine for days 4–7** (drag day median 4–7; both Claude runs dragged from day 4–5):
  - Day 4: a **contract**. A collector asks for three specific cards by day 7, and pays a premium.
  - Day 6: a **regional show** flyer. Book a table at close for 40₵, bring 6 items, and sell to a crowd that night.
  - Day 7: returning crooks (exists).
  - Each gives the middle a goal and a payoff.
- **Vintage within reach:** day 3 or 4 brings a "single old pack" estate lot at ~100–180₵, so vintage starts as a bet you can afford. The big boxes come later.
  - **Q9:** OK to add a cheap vintage tier? It affects the Balance agent's open vintage target question.
- **A safer cash floor mid-run** (21/24 Tier A runs close mid-run days broke): a small daily "regulars" income tied to stars, or a bulk-buyback offer when cash is under rent.
- **Rescan reminder and the warnings bar** (section 5).
