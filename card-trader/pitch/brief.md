# Card Trader (working title) · Brief

© 2026 justaskjoe. All rights reserved.

Solo pitch from Hunter, answered in Pitch Forge on October 31, 2026. Zach and Joe haven't answered yet, and many answers point at the reference game instead of deciding. So the mechanics below are **proposals for the crew to confirm**, written to be our own take, not a copy.

## The hook
Run a tiny card shop in a back alley of a Tokyo electronics district. People walk in with binders, shoeboxes and "my uncle's old collection". You decide what's real, what it's worth, and whether you even want to know where it came from. Then you price it, grade it, or flip it, and pray the market doesn't turn.

## Where it comes from
- **From shopkeeper sims like Probably Stolen (released Oct 28, 2026):** the shape of the loop. Stock up, appraise, haggle, sell, and keep the rent paid, with customers who aren't always what they seem and goods that may not be clean.
- **From TCG shop sims:** the variety of cards, packs and sealed product, and the collector's thrill.
- **Ours:** the actual hobby. Condition, grading, fakes, set releases and hype cycles are the systems, not decoration.

**This must not be a reskin.** Every mechanic below is built from how card collecting actually works, and we never borrow the reference game's names, characters, UI or content.

## Signature mechanic: The Loupe and Grade Day
Every card can be inspected by hand: tilt it under the lamp for surface scratches, check the edges for whitening, measure the centering, feel the card stock for a fake. Your read sets what you pay and charge. Grade Day is the test. Send your best finds to the grading service, wait a few in-game days, and find out whether your eye was right. A 10 can make your month; a 6 on a card you paid 9 money for can sink it. That reveal is the clip and the screenshot.

## Decisions
- **Player:** people who love sim games and TCGs. Streamers and their viewers matter most: the grade reveal and the "binder find" are built to be shared.
- **Platforms:** Steam PC first, Switch later. The prototype uses our phone-first web kit.
- **Perspective:** first person at the shop counter. The phone prototype uses a flat counter view (the cards and the seller in front of you); full first person comes with the PC build.
- **Modes:** solo campaign, then an endless shop with a weekly leaderboard (shop value, best grade, best flip).
- **Difficulty:** tough but fair. Money is always tight: rent rises, grading costs money and time, and one bad buy hurts. No endless-money drift.
- **Sessions:** a shop day is about 15 minutes; play one or play ten.
- **Price:** premium, upfront. No microtransactions.

## The loop (one shop day)
1. **Open up:** check the market board (prices moved overnight: a new set dropped, a card got banned, a streamer opened something big), set your display case prices, and decide what to send to grading.
2. **Buy:** sellers walk in with binders and boxes. Inspect, haggle, then buy, pass, or ask where it came from.
3. **Sell:** buyers browse the case, haggle, and sometimes try to swap a fake or a damaged card back.
4. **Close up:** pay the bills, collect any grading results, and spend on upgrades: a better lamp and loupe, more case space, a grading-service account, sealed product, a back room.

## The "probably stolen" side, made about cards
Some collections are too good to be true. A seller with a whole binder of high-end cards, cheap, who won't say where they came from:
- You can **buy it** and risk a visit from the police or the owner.
- You can **refuse** and maybe lose the deal of the month.
- You can **report** them and build a reputation that brings honest regulars.

Your reputation decides who walks in the door.

## Fakes and tricks
Fake cards, resealed booster packs, trimmed cards, and swap scams at the counter. Spotting them is a skill that improves with better tools and experience. Selling a fake knowingly is possible, but it's a reputation bomb if caught.

## World
A fictional back alley in a Tokyo electronics district, at street level under the neon. Three or four **fictional** card games, each with its own sets, rarities and fan culture (never real ones: no real card game names, characters or art). There's a rival shop across the street, a grizzled grader you deal with, and regulars with their own stories and wants lists.

**Goal:** go from a rented corner stall to owning the most trusted (or most notorious) card shop in the district. Buy out the lease before the landlord sells it under you.

## Prototype 1
One shop day, about 15 minutes:
- **Sellers and buyers:** 4 sellers (one with a too-good collection, one with a fake mixed in) and 4 buyers.
- **Inspection:** the Loupe on one card at a time (centering, edges, surface, real or fake).
- **Grading:** one grading result returning at close.
- **Card games:** 1 fictional card game with about 30 cards.
- **Pressure:** rent due at the end of the day.

**The one question it answers:** is inspecting, haggling over and grading cards fun on its own?
**Pass bar:** everyone plays a second day without being asked.

## Risks
- **Too close to the reference game:** we must design our own systems from the hobby (above) and avoid its names, look and structure. Check this at every review.
- **Real card game IP:** no real card game names, art or logos, ever. Our cards are original.
- **Crowded neighbor:** card-shop sims already exist (crew to verify which). Our angle is appraisal, grading and trust, not shelf-stocking.
- **Economy balance:** keeping money tight without making it feel unfair needs early tuning tools.
- **Scope:** first-person 3D for PC and Switch is a big step from a phone prototype. Prove the loop in 2D first.
