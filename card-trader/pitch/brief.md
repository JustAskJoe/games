# Intergalactic Card Trader (working title) · Brief

Solo pitch from Hunter, answered in Pitch Forge and approved there on October 31, 2026. Same day, Hunter shifted the theme from a Tokyo back-alley shop to space: "Happy space Akihabara". Zach and Joe haven't answered yet, and many answers pointed at the reference game instead of deciding. So the mechanics below are **proposals for the crew to confirm**, written to be our own take, not a copy.

## The hook
A cozy little card shop bolted onto a market station where the galaxy's trade lanes cross. Every species has its own card game, its own language and its own idea of what makes a card precious. Ships dock, collectors wander in with binders from a hundred worlds, and you decide what's real, what it's worth, and where in the galaxy someone would pay the most for it.

## Tone: happy space Akihabara
Warm, funny and full of wonder: neon signs in a dozen alien scripts, vending machines that sell booster packs and noodle soup, pack-hunters camping the dock, alien streamers opening boxes live. It's the joy of a hobby shop district, in space. **It is never grim, dystopian or cyberpunk.** That keeps it clearly apart from the reference game, which is set on a dark cyberpunk station.

## Where it comes from
- **From shopkeeper sims like Probably Stolen (released Oct 28, 2026):** the shape of the loop. Stock up, appraise, haggle, sell and keep the rent paid, with customers who aren't always what they seem and goods that may not be clean.
- **From TCG shop sims:** the variety of cards, packs and sealed product, and the collector's thrill.
- **Ours:** the hobby itself (condition, grading, fakes, set releases, hype) multiplied across alien cultures, plus trade lanes that make every card worth something different depending on who's docking.

**This must not be a reskin.** We never borrow the reference game's names, characters, UI, setting details or content.

## Signature mechanic: The Loupe and Grade Day
Every card can be inspected by hand: tilt it under the lamp for scratches, check the edges for wear, measure the centering, read the script, and look for an alien forger's tells. Your read sets what you pay and charge. Grade Day is the test. Send your best finds to a grading company, wait a few station days, and find out whether your eye was right. A perfect grade can make your month; a bad one on a card you overpaid for can sink it. That reveal is the clip and the screenshot.

## What the space theme adds to the loop
- **Trade lanes:** each day a different mix of ships docks, from different species and hubs. Who's docking decides who walks in and what they want. A card that's junk in one system is a grail two systems over, so buying low from one culture and selling high to another is the core money game.
- **Many card games:** each civilization has its own card game, with its own rarities, superstitions and collectors. You can't know them all. Learning a new game is progression: its players start trusting you, and its cards stop being a gamble.
- **Languages:** cards arrive in scripts you can't read. Decoding a card yourself, or paying a translator, is part of appraisal. Untranslated cards sell cheap, and that's where the best "binder finds" hide.
- **Grading companies:** several graders, each with its own scale, fees, turnaround and reputation. One is fast but bribable; one is slow, but its top grade is gospel in the core worlds. Where you grade a card is a real choice.
- **Probably stolen, space edition:** relic cards smuggled out of a system that bans their sale, sacred cards one species considers stolen property, and a seller with a too-good binder who won't say which ship they came off. You can buy and risk customs and an angry faction, refuse and maybe miss the deal of the month, or report them and build trust. Your standing with each faction decides who docks at your door.
- **Fakes with alien tells:** a holo pattern that only shows under one star's light, ink that glows wrong, card stock from the wrong world. New Loupe filters are upgrades.

## Decisions
- **Player:** people who love sim games and card games. Streamers and their viewers matter most: the grade reveal and the binder find are built to be shared.
- **Platforms:** Steam PC first, Switch later. The prototype uses our phone-first web kit.
- **Perspective:** first person at the shop counter. The phone prototype uses a flat counter view (the card and the customer in front of you); full first person comes with the PC build.
- **Modes:** a solo campaign, then an endless shop with a weekly leaderboard (shop value, best grade, best flip).
- **Difficulty:** tough but fair. Money is always tight: dock rent rises, grading costs money and time, and one bad buy hurts. No endless-money drift.
- **Sessions:** a station day is about 15 minutes; play one or play ten.
- **Price:** premium, upfront. No microtransactions.

## The loop (one station day)
1. **Open up:** see which ships dock today and what their cultures want, set your case prices, and choose what to send to which grader.
2. **Buy:** sellers come in with binders and crates. Inspect, decode, haggle, then buy, pass, or ask where it came from.
3. **Sell:** buyers browse, haggle, and sometimes try a swap scam or a fake.
4. **Close up:** pay dock rent, collect grading results, and spend on upgrades: Loupe filters, a translator, more case space, grader accounts, sealed product from other hubs.

## World
A market station where the trade lanes cross, its main strip lined with card shops, pack vending machines and food stalls. Three or four **fictional** card games from different species, each with its own sets, script and fan culture (never real card games, names or art). There's a rival shop across the strip, a grader's office with a grumpy appraiser, and regulars from all over the galaxy with their own stories and wants lists.

**Goal:** go from a rented corner stall to the most trusted (or most notorious) card shop on the station, buy out your dock lease, then open shops at other hubs.

## Prototype 1
One station day, about 15 minutes:
- **Ships:** 2 species docking, with 2 fictional card games (about 20 cards each).
- **Sellers:** 4, one of them with a too-good binder and one with a fake mixed in.
- **Buyers:** 4.
- **The Loupe:** centering, edges, surface, real or fake, and reading the script.
- **One untranslated card** worth a fortune if you can read it.
- **Graders:** 2 grading companies, with one result back at close.
- **Pressure:** dock rent due at the end of the day.

**The one question it answers:** is inspecting, decoding, haggling over and grading cards fun on its own?
**Pass bar:** everyone plays a second day without being asked.

## Risks
- **Too close to the reference game:** a shop on a space station shares its setting. Keep the tone happy rather than dystopian, and build the identity from trade lanes and many cultures, which it doesn't have. Check this at every review.
- **Real card game IP:** no real card game names, art or logos, ever. Every card and game is ours.
- **Crowded neighbor:** card-shop sims already exist (crew to verify which). Our angle is appraisal, grading, languages and trade lanes, not shelf-stocking.
- **Content load:** several card games, scripts and species is a lot of art and writing. Start with two and keep the systems doing the work.
- **Economy balance:** tight money that never feels unfair needs early tuning tools.
- **Scope:** first-person 3D for PC and Switch is a big step from a phone prototype. Prove the loop in 2D first.
