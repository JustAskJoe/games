# Wormsbound
Genre: Other · Mode: team

## The idea

### I1 Working title (or a few candidates).
- Hunter: (no answer)
- You: Crater Club

### I2 Pitch it in one sentence: "It's a ___ where you…"
- Hunter: (no answer)
- You: It's a turn-based artillery brawler where you and up to three friends on one screen aim, angle, and lob shells across destructible terrain to blow each other (and the ground) to bits.

### I3 The hook: what would a player say to a friend after their first session?
- Hunter: (no answer)
- You: "My dud bounced off a cliff, sat there ticking, and then took out BOTH of us. Rematch, right now."

### I4 What's the mashup? From game or genre A we take…, from B we take…
- Hunter: (no answer)
- You: From Worms we take: fully destructible terrain, a wacky weapon arsenal, turn-based squads, and couch-chaos energy.
From GunBound we take: angle + power aiming, wind you have to read, distinct character/mobile classes with their own shot types, and the turn-delay system.
On top: modern 2.5D graphics, physics debris, and big-moment polish (slow-mo, replays).

### I5 Why now? What changed (tech, platform, trend, audience) that makes this the right time?
- Hunter: (no answer)
- You: A modern take on a PvP classic that blows up to be a fun casual night with the boys.
Couch/local multiplayer is having a moment again (party games, Steam Remote Play Together), and modern engines make destructible terrain, dynamic lighting, and physics cheap to build. The artillery classics still look and feel dated, so there's room for a polished, modern version. (Stats to be sourced before slides.)

### I6 What's annoying or missing in this genre today that we fix?
- Hunter: (no answer)
- You: Today: dated graphics, slow turns and long waits, clunky menus, too many weapons to learn up front, matches that drag on.
Us: modern 2.5D visuals, a turn timer and snappy pacing, drag-to-aim controls, a tight starter arsenal, 5–10 minute rounds, and a one-button instant rematch.

### I7 Three games it should feel closest to, and what we steal from each.
- Hunter: (no answer)
- You: Worms, GunBound, Pocket Tanks, ShellShock Live.

What’s new compared with them:

Graphics:
Smooth 2.5D maps with dynamic lighting.
Explosions that blow chunks and debris out of the ground.
Weather that you can see, like wind-blown dust and rain.
Screen shake, slow motion on direct hits, and replays of the best shots.
Controls: drag to aim, with a clean, modern interface that works with mouse, touch or controller.

### I8 What should it never be? List the anti-goals.
- Hunter: (no answer)
- You: Never pay-to-win.
Never a slow, 30-minute turn grind.
Not a realistic military sim.
Not 60 weapons on day one.
Not online-only: it has to be fun on one couch.

## How it plays

### T2 Camera and perspective.
- Hunter: (no answer)
- You: Side view (note: 2.5D side view with parallax and dynamic lighting; camera follows the shell in flight.)

### T3 The core loop in 4 steps (prep → wave → reward → upgrade, or your own).
- Hunter: (no answer)
- You: 1. Move & aim: walk your unit, set angle and power, read the wind.
2. Fire: pick a weapon and launch.
3. Boom: terrain craters, damage lands, live duds start ticking.
4. React: next player's turn — dodge, dig in, or lob a live shell back. Last one standing wins the round.

### T8 What does the player physically do moment to moment?
- Hunter: (no answer)
- You: Aim / shoot themselves (note: Move, set angle + power, choose weapon, lob back live duds.)

### T10 Economy: what does the player earn and spend, and on what?
- Hunter: (no answer)
- You: v1 (local PvP): no persistent economy. Each match starts with a loadout; supply crates drop mid-match with bonus weapons and health.
Later: earn XP/coins from matches to unlock cosmetics (skins, shell trails, explosion styles) and new maps — never stat boosts.

### T11 The signature mechanic: the one thing no other game in the genre does.
- Hunter: (no answer)
- You: Live duds: shells that land without exploding stay live and keep ticking. On your turn you can fire a new shot or lob a live shell back. A miss becomes everyone's problem, which gives the classic formula a fresh twist.

### T12 What happens when an enemy leaks or you lose a level? How fast can you retry?
- Hunter: (no answer)
- You: Lose a round and you're back in under 10 seconds with a one-button rematch. Matches are best-of-3 (or 5) rounds so one bad shot doesn't end the night. Map and turn order shuffle each round.

### T14 How do we teach it in the first 3 minutes? (Lesson 1 to 4)
- Hunter: (no answer)
- You: Lesson 1: Move and aim (angle + power) at a still target.
Lesson 2: Wind — curve a shot around it.
Lesson 3: Terrain — blast a path, avoid falls and water.
Lesson 4: Live duds — lob one back at a dummy.
Then straight into a 2-player match.

## World & progression

### W1 Setting and premise in two or three sentences.
- Hunter: (no answer)
- You: Rival crews of scrappy demolition misfits face off in the Crater Club, an underground league fought on floating islands, junkyards and rooftops. The only rule: last one standing takes the trophy. Every match reshapes the arena.

### W2 What is the player's goal, and why should they care?
- Hunter: (no answer)
- You: Knock out every opposing unit to win the round, then the match. Players care because it's bragging rights against the friends sitting next to them.

### W3 Overall structure.
- Hunter: (no answer)
- You: Endless + leaderboards (note: v1 is match-based local PvP: pick a map, play best-of-X, track wins per player. No campaign yet.)

### W4 What carries over between levels or runs?
- Hunter: (no answer)
- You: Cosmetics only (note: Plus unlocking new maps and weapons over time.)

### W5 How many maps or levels for a first release?
- Hunter: (no answer)
- You: 5

### W6 Characters: hero, villain, narrator, rivals. Who talks to the player?
- Hunter: (no answer)
- You: Playable crew: 4–6 characters with distinct looks and voiced quips.
An announcer/MC hosts the Crater Club and calls out big shots.
No villain — your friends are the rivals.

### W7 What changes from level to level to keep it fresh?
- Hunter: (no answer)
- You: Different arenas (biome, shape, hazards like water, lava, wind gusts), random wind each turn, random crate drops, terrain destroyed differently every match, and sudden-death events (rising water).

### W8 Endgame: why play the 100th hour?
- Hunter: (no answer)
- You: Mastering trick shots, playing with the same friend group, new maps/weapons/cosmetics, and later ranked online play, a map editor, and custom rules.

### A2 Three art pillars (adjectives) and a palette mood.
- Hunter: (no answer)

### A3 Visual references: games, films, artists, links.
- Hunter: (no answer)
- You: Worms W.M.D., GunBound, ShellShock Live, Brawlhalla, Fall Guys, Rocket League (polish and juice), Ratchet & Clank, Pixar shorts.

### A4 How does a player read the important game pieces at a glance?
- Hunter: (no answer)
- You: Bold team colors with outlines on every unit; HP bars over heads; aim arc and wind arrow always on screen; live duds glow and pulse with a countdown number; terrain has a clear foreground silhouette against a blurred background.

### A5 Characters / creatures look and how their state shows.
- Hunter: (no answer)
- You: Chunky, expressive characters with oversized helmets and goggles. State shows through animation: healthy = bouncy idle; low HP = smoke, limp, cracked gear; their turn = spotlight and glowing outline; knocked out = cartoon poof into a gravestone.

### A6 Environment look: biomes, ground, the path itself.
- Hunter: (no answer)
- You: Floating islands, junkyards, frozen peaks, volcano, city rooftops. Ground is layered soil and rock that craters with debris chunks and dust. Parallax backgrounds, dynamic lighting, visible weather (rain, wind-blown dust).

### A7 UI feel.
- Hunter: (no answer)
- You: Chunky arcade

### A8 Music and sound direction. What should a perfect wave clear sound like?
- Hunter: (no answer)
- You: Upbeat, punchy soundtrack (funk/rock with electronic edges). Big bassy explosions, a whistle on long shots, crowd cheers and announcer calls on knockouts. A perfect shot: whistle → a beat of silence → deep boom → slow-mo → crowd roar.
