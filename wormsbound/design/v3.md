# Wormsbound v3: kits, movement and a map worth fighting over

© 2026 justaskjoe. All rights reserved.

**Status: a design proposal from Hunter (with Claude), Nov 16, 2026. Nothing here is built.** It sits on top of v2 ([`v2.md`](v2.md), pull request #107). Louiemark leads Wormsbound and decides what goes ahead; the questions for him are at the end. Every number is a first guess to be tuned by playing.

## Why

Hunter played v2 and said three things:

1. **The weapons meld together.** He's right, and the code shows why: all 30 character shots and all 14 gear items are the same action, "lob a shell that makes a blast", with different numbers (blast radius 3 to 6, damage 24 to 56). More weapons made more of the same.
2. **Movement isn't good.** It's walk-only, one cell at a time, with no jump. Getting somewhere is never a decision.
3. **You can't tell the characters apart.** The v2 sprites are one round body with ten different hats.

The goal stays Louiemark's: **a "League feel" brought to an artillery game, not as hardcore.** In League terms, v2 gave every champion three versions of the same basic attack. v3 gives each one a kit.

## What v3 is, in one paragraph

Weapons go away. Every character has a **kit**: a passive, a basic shot, a signature ability, a move ability and an ultimate, where each slot does a *different kind of thing*. Everyone can **jump**, and can **move a little after shooting**. Characters have **six visible stats**. The map gets **shrines, a Relic and movers** to fight over, and you **level up during a match** to shape your kit. The ten characters become **ten different creatures** with outlines you can tell apart as black shapes.

## 1. Kits replace weapons

| Slot | Key | What it is | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Passive | none | Always on. The character's identity in one sentence. | none |
| Basic shot | 1 | The artillery skill shot: drag, aim, read the wind. | unlimited |
| Ability | 2 | The signature trick. Never just "a bigger shell". | cooldown, 2 or 3 of your own turns |
| Move ability | 3 | How this character gets around. | cooldown, 2 or 3 turns |
| Ultimate | 4 | Today's SS. | charge, as now (combos and crates still feed it) |

**What goes:** ammo counts, Shot 2 as a second shell, and the gear weapon slot (all 14 items). Cooldowns replace ammo: nothing runs out, it just isn't ready.

**What stays:** live duds and the lob. Every basic shot can still be fired as a dud, and anyone can walk up and lob one back. It's the one rule nothing else in the genre has, so it belongs to everybody.

**The rule that stops melding:** every ability is tagged with one **verb**, and a kit can't use the same verb twice.

| Verb | Means | Colour in effects and icons |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Blast | damage in an area | orange |
| Shove | push or pull units | white |
| Build | add ground | green |
| Dig | remove ground without a blast | brown |
| Zone | an area that stays for turns | purple |
| Mend | heal or shield | pink |
| Mark | make a target easier to hurt or find | yellow |
| Time | change the turn order (turn delay) | blue |
| Move | relocate yourself or a crewmate | teal |

## 2. Stats

Six, shown as bars on the select screen and in the unit panel. Classes get clear shapes.

| Stat | What it does | Range now | Tank | Scout | Gunner | Engineer | Controller |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Health | hit points | 90 to 110 | high | mid | mid | high | low |
| Armor | cuts damage taken | 0 to 12% | high | none | none | low | none |
| Power | multiplies damage dealt | new, 0.9 to 1.15 | low | mid | high | low | mid |
| Move | size of the movement bar | 9 to 22 steps | low | high | mid | mid | mid |
| Jump | how high a hop goes | new, 3 to 8 cells | low | high | mid | low | mid |
| Weight | cuts how far shoves move you | new, 0.6 to 1.4 | high | low | mid | high | low |

Items (section 4) and level-ups (section 5) move these numbers. That's what makes them worth showing.

## 3. Movement

| Now | v3 |
| --- | --- |
| Walk only, one cell a tap. | Hold to walk smoothly; a **movement bar** drains. |
| Climbs ledges 2 cells high; anything more is a wall. | **Jump** (Up, or W): a forward hop about 3 across and as high as your Jump stat. **Back-jump** (double-tap): higher, shorter, backwards. Each costs a fifth of the bar. |
| Your turn ends the moment you fire. | **Retreat:** after firing you keep a third of the bar and 3 seconds to use it. Shoot, then hide. |
| No way across a gap. | One **move ability** each (section 1). |

Fall damage stays, so height is still a risk. Turn delay stays too: walking and jumping add a little delay, abilities add more, so a turn full of tricks still means a later next turn.

**How it stays in step online:** a jump is one action (`{k:'jump', back}`) that the engine plays out with the same physics as a shoved unit, so replays, checksums and the room server work the way they do now.

## 4. Things on the map

Three kinds. All placed by the map generator from the match's seed.

- **Shrines** (2 or 3 a map, on high or awkward ground). Start your turn standing on one and that unit gets its buff until its next turn: **Power** (+20% damage), **Guard** (-25% damage taken) or **Haste** (+50% movement). A reason to take and hold a place.
- **The Relic** (one a match). Lands in the middle once everyone has had about three turns, with a countdown shown from the start. It has health; the team that lands the last hit takes it: **every unit on the team gets a notch of ultimate charge and +10% Power for the rest of the round.** This is the fight both sides walk into.
- **Movers**, by theme: **geysers** (launch whoever is on them at the start of each round of turns), **zip lines** (ride between two posts for a few steps of movement), **teleporter pairs**. Two or three kinds to start; each theme gets one.

**Crates stay** but change: health and ultimate charge as now, and **items** in place of gear weapons. An item is a small permanent boost for that unit: a stat (+10% Power, +15 Health, +30% Move, +2 Jump) or a little passive (first shove each round ignored; cooldowns one turn shorter). One item slot to start.

## 5. The metagame

**During a match: levels.**
- Units earn experience from damage dealt, crates, shrines held and the Relic.
- Two level-ups a round, at about a third and two thirds of an average match.
- At each one, **pick one of two upgrades** for your kit. Example for Brick: "Bulwark is 2 cells taller" or "Charge shoves 50% further". Twenty characters' worth of choices is 40 upgrades; start with one pair per ability.
- Levels reset each round. No shop, no gold, no last-hitting.

**Before a match: the crew.** With real roles, composition matters: a crew with no Engineer can't cross a gap someone blew open; a crew with no Tank can't hold a shrine. In Squads you already pick each unit, and that screen gets the stat bars and kit cards.

**Between matches: nothing yet, on purpose.** Bans, ranked play, mastery and unlocks all need accounts and a player base. They're listed so the design leaves room, not so anyone builds them.

**Why this isn't "as hardcore":** four buttons, no build to memorise before you start, every choice is one of two and comes with a picture, and a bad pick costs you a round, not a 40-minute game.

## 6. Art and animation

The job is **telling things apart at a glance**: who that is, what they just did, and where it will land.

**Characters.** Ten creatures, one per character: armadillo, boar, ferret, frog, gecko, salamander, mole, beaver, heron, cloud sheep. Concept sheet: [`../art/concepts/v3-lineup.png`](../art/concepts/v3-lineup.png), drawn by `art/tools/lineup.py`.
- **Class sets the shape:** tanks wide and square, scouts lean and tilted forward, gunners upright with a big weapon, engineers low with a tool, controllers tall or floating.
- **One prop each, tied to the kit:** Brick's cannon, Tusk's ram helmet, Zip's scarf and goggles, Hopper's slingshot, Volt's coil pack, Flint's mask and tank, Digger's claws and lamp, Patch's wrench and hard hat, Gale's kite wing, Nimbus's rain cloud.
- **Team colour is only the band, scarf or panel,** plus the outline glow in play. In v2 it tints the whole body, which is half of why they look alike.
- **Size:** 48 × 48 art pixels (v2's were 32). In today's game they're drawn at 1.25x so they don't dwarf a unit's hit circle; v3's movement work decides the final size.

**Frames per character:** idle (2, with a habit of their own), walk (4), jump (2), aim (1), fire (2), cast (2), hurt (1), knocked out (2). About 16 a character, 160 in all, still drawn in code.

**Abilities.** Each gets an icon, an effect in its verb's colour (section 1), and a **preview while aiming**: the wall's outline, the bridge's span, the hook's line, the pounce's landing spot. Cooldowns show as a dimmed icon with a number.

**Status (Nov 16):** Hunter approved the lineup, and all ten are drawn with all 16 frames (`art/tools/creatures.py`, sheet in [`../art/concepts/v3-creatures.png`](../art/concepts/v3-creatures.png)). The game uses idle, walk, aim, fire, hurt and knocked out now; jump and cast wait for phase 1. Still to do: ability icons, effects and aim previews.

**The world (Nov 16):** the ground is now painted smooth from the same rules grid (`prototype/look.js`), and each theme has a generated background picture. Motion and sound are in too (debris that lands, dust, a hold and a nudge of the view on big hits, wind you can see and hear, everything at the ground's pixel size). Planned next: drawing on the graphics card for real light and shadow.

**Order of work:** the lineup is approved first (this sheet), then idle and walk for all ten, then the rest of the frames, then ability effects. The Artist's lead plan (`art/lead-plan.md`, pull request #108) should be updated to this before any art request is filed.

## 8. How it gets built

Three pull requests, each stacked on the last, **each played by a person before the next starts**.

1. **Movement and kits.** Jump, retreat, the movement bar, all ten kits with cooldowns, stats. Gear removed; crates give health and charge only for now. The biggest of the three.
2. **Map and levels.** Shrines, the Relic, movers, items in crates, level-ups.
3. **Art and animation.** The ten creatures with full frames, ability icons, effects and aim previews. Can run alongside 1 and 2 because it only touches `art/` until the last step.

## Risks

- **Bots will play this worse than people.** They can aim a shell. Knowing when a wall, a bridge or a jump pad is worth a turn is much harder. The balance sim stays useful for damage and match length, and becomes a weak guide for everything else. Playtests carry more weight than in v2.
- **Nobody has reviewed v2.** v3 replaces v2's gear and changes its crates. Louiemark should read #107 as the foundation, not the final shape.
- **It's a lot at once.** Ten kits is 40 abilities. If phase 1 slips, the fallback is five kits (one per class) with the other five following.
- **Build abilities can wall a match shut.** Walls and bridges need limits (they crumble after a few turns, or cap at two per unit) or two Engineers will turtle forever.
- **Phones.** Jumping and retreating on touch need buttons that don't exist yet.
