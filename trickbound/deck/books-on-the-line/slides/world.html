THE WORLD · THE LINE

The trains run as long as the Long Table is played.

A made-up country of mining towns on one railway. The mines are gone, one train runs a week, and every depot has a card room.

A hundred-year folk belief keeps people going. This is the last season. The Line closes after this year’s Long Table, whoever wins.

The Long Table: a hundred years ago, when the Railway first tried to close the Line, miners from every town played Books against the Railway’s men at one long table in the Terminus hall, a seat for every town. They won and the trains kept running. Every year since, each town sends its best pair. Nobody really thinks a card game runs a railroad, but nobody wants to be the year it isn’t played. The setting is fully invented on purpose: no real-world history to answer to, and room for lore. Tone: warm and funny at the table, melancholy on the platform. The emotional engine is a place past its prime, and a card game people hold onto for hope even though they half know better.
