# Trickbound: Books on the Line — v3 scope

© 2026 F Lane d/b/a One Fifty Labs. All rights reserved.

**One line:** Pokémon TCG on the Game Boy, but it's a Spades-style card game called *Books*, played in the card rooms of a dying railway's last season.

Decisions (F, 2026-10-16):
- **Name: Trickbound is the series; game one is *Books on the Line*; the Long Table is its finale.** Game two could be the seaside "Off-Season" setting, adapted, with its own card game. Working titles, no name search yet.
- Setting is fully invented: **the Line**. No real-world history or culture to answer to.
- In-world the game is **Books**. Rules are standard Spades underneath.
- 9 depot towns, 3 junction cities, a terminus finale.
- Seated, Inscryption-style table; Habbo-style isometric rooms for the light adventure; Coffee Talk-style conversations (on the train, between hands).
- **Disco Elysium influence, two things only: art and voices.** Painterly portraits; every lantern critter is a voice in your head at the table (see 3b).
- Partners come and go with the story, not by player choice; some come back changed, some end up across the table.
- Familiars come from growth, stones, quests, badges and hidden conditions; 24–32 total.
- Cards are a second, cosmetic collection: packs, holos, hidden promos.
- Story is premium ($9.99, first town free), no real money inside it. Multiplayer may sell cosmetics; never real-money packs.
- **Trickbound and Cardboard Cartel stay separate games.**

## 1. The world: the Line
A made-up country of mining and river towns strung along one railway. The mines closed a generation ago; the Line runs on fumes, one train a week. Every depot has a card room in the old waiting hall.

**The hope people cling to:** a hundred years ago, when the Railway first tried to close the Line, miners from every town rode to Terminus and played Books against the Railway's men at one long table down the station hall, a seat for every town. The miners won and the trains kept running. Every year since, each town sends its best pair to play the **Long Table**: part tournament, part ritual. Nobody really thinks a card game runs a railroad, but nobody wants to be the year it isn't played.

**The news:** this is the last season. The Railway is closing the Line after this year's Long Table, whoever wins.

**Tone:** warm, funny at the table, melancholy on the platform. Lamplight, steam, frost on windows, card rooms full of people who have nowhere else to be and don't want to be anywhere else.

## 2. Books (the game)
- Standard Spades rules. A trick is a **book**; spades are **night** (trump).
- House rules are local folk tradition, one per depot:
  | Folk name | Real rule |
  |---|---|
  | The miner's count | Mirror: bid exactly your spade count |
  | Going dark | Blind nil |
  | The long night | Nil Night: one partner per team must bid nil |
  | Conductor & Brakeman | Big and Little Joker as top trumps (jokers & deuces) |
  | Short shift | Game to 250 |
  | The old way | Classic: no familiars, no helpers |

## 3. Lantern critters (familiar lore)
- Miners carried **lantern critters** in their decks for luck: little spirits that live in the cards and only wake at a real table.
- That's why familiars can bend the rules: the critter is doing it.
- You arrive with your grandmother's old deck. Its critters are asleep. The first one wakes in Cinder Hollow.
- **The Stationmaster's critter went dark years ago.** It's why he stopped believing. It wakes in the finale.
- Families map to the Line: Ember (the Pits, kilns and forges), Tide (the River Reach), Grove (the High Plains), Dusk (night, the terminus, legends).

## 3b. Voices (Disco Elysium-inspired)
F's call (2026-10-16): take two things from Disco Elysium, art and voices. Nothing else (no walls of text, no RPG skill checks, no failure spirals). Books is the game; the voices season it.

**Every lantern critter is a voice.** The familiars you carry talk to you at the table, each with its own personality, and that *is* the coach/helper system, made diegetic:
| Critter | Voice | Speaks about | Sample line |
|---|---|---|---|
| Owlet (Dusk) | quiet, precise, a little smug | counting, what's still out | "Seven nights still out. Tasha's been void in Tide since book two." |
| Salamander (Ember) | hot-headed, loves a risk | going big, leading early, setting them | "Lead the night. Make them sweat." |
| Koi (Tide) | calm, second-guessing | the bid, adjusting, reading the flow | "You bid four. The table says three. Rethink?" |
| Badger (Grove) | gruff, protective | bags, safety, covering partner | "That's enough books. Duck. Bags bite." |
| Raven | sly gossip | opponents' hands, tells | "Marcus is sitting on the boss. He always touches his collar." |
| Mouse | nervous, sneaky | nil, hiding, the odd play | "Nobody's watching your Grove. Slip it." |
| Moth-fox, courts, legends | bigger, stranger voices | late game, the story | Legends speak rarely, and it matters when they do |

- Voices appear as short lines beside your hand, one at a time, never a wall. Players choose how chatty: off / quiet (key moments only) / full.
- **Voices can disagree.** Salamander says lead the night; Badger says duck. You choose. That's the fun of it, and it teaches strategy without a tutorial.
- The voices you hear depend on which critters you've woken and which familiar you brought, so collecting changes what advice you get.
- Lore fit: the critters live in your grandmother's deck; as they wake, you hear more of them. The Stationmaster stopped hearing his years ago.
- Mechanically the voices reuse the existing helper logic (sure-book count, suggested play, bag warnings, Owlet/Raven info). It's writing and presentation, not a new system.

**Art:** expressionist painterly portraits for people (loose brushwork, lamplight on faces; see the Stationmaster mood image), crisp Art Nouveau for cards so they read in a blink. Voices get small painted critter icons in the same painterly style.

## 4. Map: 9 towns, 3 junctions, 1 terminus
| Leg | Region / family | Depot towns | Junction city |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The Pits** · Ember | Cinder Hollow, Lamplight, Copperbank | **Ironmere** |
| 2 | **The River Reach** · Tide | Millrace, Pike's Ferry, Sallow | **Confluence** |
| 3 | **The High Plains** · Grove | Windrow, Saltgrass, Last Water | **Meridian** |
| — | Dusk | — | **Terminus** (the Long Table) |

- **A depot town:** one isometric room (waiting-hall card room) with a counter that sells packs and stones, a quest board, regulars, and a back-room table run by the house team with that town's house rule.
- **A junction city:** a three-table bracket against stronger teams with signature familiars. Win → a junction badge (a brass ticket punch), a familiar, a junction promo card.
- **Travel:** the train map. Between stops you sit in the **conversation car**, where partner and rival scenes happen (Coffee Talk-style). Occasional events on the train: a game in the dining car, a stowaway, a lost ticket.

## 6. Story in three acts
- **Act 1 — The Pits.** Arrive at Cinder Hollow. Dee teaches you Books and the first critter wakes. Work up the Pits to Ironmere. Meet Marcus and Tasha, who are playing for the prize money to keep their town's depot open. After Ironmere, Dee gets a letter and takes the next train home. Not your call.
- **Act 2 — The River Reach.** Marcus and Tasha fall out over money; Marcus ends up across the table from you. Learn what the Stationmaster is doing with the rolling stock. At Confluence, Tasha signs with the Stationmaster.
- **Act 3 — The High Plains.** A new partner (Tally, the Foreman or Jo, depending on choices). The last towns are half-empty. At Meridian, rumor that Dee is coming back.
- **Finale — Terminus.** The Long Table. Dee steps off the last train, changed, with a Dusk legend. You and Dee against the Stationmaster and Tasha. His critter wakes mid-game, and you hear its voice for the first time.
- **Ending:** the Line closes either way. What the ending decides is what the hope was ever about: a travelling table that keeps Books going in every town, the Stationmaster's pension plan, or something the player chooses.

## 7. Partners (rules)
- The story decides who sits across from you. People leave at story beats; some return stronger or different; some end up on the other side.
- Each partner has their own bond track and duo moves; you lose those when they leave and regain changed versions when they return.
- Every new partner plays differently, which keeps 9 towns of Books fresh.
- After the story, free play lets you pick any partner you've met.

## 8. Collection A — Familiars (mechanics)
- 24–32 at launch. Sim balance gate for every one (young +2..+6 win points; grown once a night; court/legend per game).
- Lines evolve young → grown → court → legend, with a few branches.
- Each familiar is also a **voice** (3b): collecting one adds its personality to your table.
- Sources:
  | Source | Example |
  |---|---|
  | Growth | Owlet → Raven after 3 winning hands |
  | Stones | Moonstone (from a Pits pack): Owlet → Moth-fox instead of Raven |
  | Quests | Pike's Ferry regular: "Make a nil at my table and I'll give you my Frog" |
  | Badges | Ironmere badge → the Foundry's Kilnback |
  | Hidden | Win a hand where every book was won by a 2–5 → a secret Mouse appears |
- One familiar per table; tier caps per region; partner brings their own.

## 9. Collection B — Cards (cosmetic)
- **52-slot deck**; you choose the version of each card. Never affects play.
- **Pip cards are critter pips:** the 7 of Ember shows seven little moles. One critter sprite per family per stage, laid out per rank.
- **Sets:**
  | Set | Look |
  |---|---|
  | Railway Base | the old company deck, cream and gilt |
  | The Pits | etched tin, soot and ember glow |
  | The River Reach | woven patterns, river blues |
  | The High Plains | stamped ticket stock, wheat and sky |
  | Terminus | night-printed, lamp-lit foil |
- **Rarities:** common, uncommon, rare, **lamp-lit** (holo), full-art, gilded. Animated foil on tilt.
- **Promos:** each junction badge has a fixed promo. Hidden promos for feats (made blind nil → "Going Dark Deuce"; all 13 books → "Full Shift Ace"; dining-car mini-game → "Dining Car Ace"). Promos never come from packs.
- **Misprints:** ultra-rare oddities from a broken press in Copperbank.
- **Packs:** 5 cards, one rare-or-better slot. Each depot stocks its region's set plus Base.
- **Binder** with provenance: "pulled at Lamplight, week 3."

## 10. Economy, modes and money
- Story cash from back-room table stakes, quests and selling duplicates at the counter. Spend on packs, stones, junction entry fees, cosmetic table items. No real money in story.
- Tuning goal: about a pack per town plus extras; full set completion takes a replay.

| Mode | Price | Real money |
|---|---|---|
| Story (the Line) | $9.99, Cinder Hollow free as demo | None |
| Multiplayer | Free | Direct cosmetics, optional season pass; never real-money packs |
| Classic ("the old way") | Free | None |

## 11. Presentation
- **Seated table** (default), layered 2D with parallax; partner's face across, opponents at the sides; reactions on bids, tells on nil; critter voices beside your hand.
- **Flat top-down table** as fast mode.
- **Isometric depot rooms** (Habbo-style) for towns; the **conversation car** for story scenes; the **train map** for travel.
- Painterly portraits (Disco Elysium-lite) for people; crisp Art Nouveau for cards.

## 12. Can the cards be as cool as Pokémon's?
The feel, yes; the nostalgia, no. We match it with a distinctive look (Art Nouveau critter cards), regional sets by different artists, shimmering lamp-lit foils, provenance stories, and showing your deck in multiplayer. Critter pips keep the 2–10 cards cheap so the budget goes to courts, Aces, foils and promos. Rough launch art: ~200 illustrations, roughly $40k–$100k at indie rates. AI mood art stays reference-only.

## 13. Multiplayer (phase two)
Small Node server, room codes, host-authoritative, rejoin, spectators. Headline mode: two humans each with an AI partner. Your story deck and familiars come with you. Later: your own card room (the Habbo club idea).

## 15. Risks and open questions
- Scope: the full game is a small team for ~2 years; each prototype proves one new thing.
- Loot-box creep: real-money packs stay out entirely.
- Art cost and consistency across regional artists; needs an art director early.
- Players may resent losing a partner; returns and free play soften it, and it's the point of the story.
- Voices need a strong writer; placeholder lines are fine for the prototype.
- Open: the country's name; whether the player's grandmother is a known Long Table champion; how dark the ending is allowed to be.
